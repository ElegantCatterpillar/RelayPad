Install

RelayPad runs on Windows 10 or 11 (x64). You need a USB-C cable that carries data, a Bluetooth adapter, or both.

  1. Download RelayPad_x.y.z_x64-setup.exe from the latest release.
  2. Run it. RelayPad installs for your user and opens the first-run setup.
  3. Follow the setup to add the drivers, then connect your controller.

The installer is not code signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may say "unknown publisher". Choose More info, then Run anyway. Updates are signed, and RelayPad checks that signature before installing one.

First-run setup

The first time it opens, RelayPad checks what your PC needs. Nothing is installed without your OK.

ViGEmBus driver
Required. It creates the virtual Xbox 360 or DualShock 4 controller your games see.
HidHide driver
Recommended. It hides the real controller from every app except RelayPad, so games do not see two controllers. You can add it later in Settings.
Bluetooth adapter
Needed for wireless play. If Bluetooth is off, turn it on in Windows settings.
Controller
Plug one in or put it in pairing mode; the next section shows how for each model.

Each driver comes from its official installer. RelayPad shows the file, where it comes from and its size, checks its SHA-256 before running it, and Windows asks for administrator permission. Some driver installs need a Windows restart to finish.

Last, RelayPad asks whether it may keep a usage report on this PC. You can change your answer later in Settings → General → Diagnostics.

Connect a controller

While no controller is connected, the main window shows how each model connects. More pairing info opens the full steps for your model.

Switch 2 Pro Controller

  • USB: plug it in. It connects within seconds and pairs with this PC, so it can come back over Bluetooth later.
  • Bluetooth: keep RelayPad open and hold the small sync button on the top edge, next to the USB-C port, until the player lights sweep. Do not add it in Windows Bluetooth settings: RelayPad pairs it itself.

Joy-Con 2

  • Joy-Con 2 have no USB port, so they need Bluetooth.
  • Detach the Joy-Con from the console or the grip, then hold the sync button on its rail until the lights sweep. Like the Pro Controller, it does not go through Windows Bluetooth settings.
  • Each Joy-Con connects and waits without a player. To use two as one controller, hold ZL on the left one and ZR on the right one together. To play one alone held sideways, press SL and SR on it together.
  • Separate in the main window splits a pair again. The pair is not remembered: it ends when either Joy-Con disconnects.

Switch Pro Controller

  • USB: plug it in; it connects within seconds.
  • Bluetooth: this one pairs through Windows. Hold the sync button until the lights sweep, then in Windows choose Add device → Bluetooth → Pro Controller. Once Windows lists it as paired, RelayPad connects it on its own.

A controller paired to a Switch console goes back to it when the console is on nearby. Turn the console off or move it away before pairing with your PC. A charge-only USB cable is not detected.

What the main window shows

Each connected controller shows its player (P1 to P4), battery, connection (USB or Bluetooth), live sticks and buttons, and its firmware and serial. Identify makes it vibrate briefly, Diagnose tests the connection, vibration, battery and sticks, and Forget removes a Bluetooth pairing.

Players

Up to four controllers play at once, each on its own player. A fifth one waits until a player is free.

  • Use the player menu on a controller to move it to a free player or swap it with another controller.
  • A controller that disconnects gets its player back when it returns.
  • All controllers shows players P1 to P4 together, plus synced controllers that still need a player, with how to bring them in.

Profiles

A profile holds how a controller behaves in games. Profiles have names you choose, and each controller type has its own. A profile loads when its controller connects, and the built-in one keeps the factory settings: changing it saves a new profile.

Output
Xbox 360 (recommended) or DualShock 4 for games that expect a PlayStation controller. Rumble on or off, rumble strength, player lights that follow the player, and Test vibration to feel it before you save.
Calibration
Guided stick calibration (center, range, review), inner and outer deadzones, invert X or Y, and response curves: Linear, Precise or Aggressive. Calibration is saved for that physical controller; the factory calibration stored on the controller is never changed.
Buttons
Pick what each button sends to the game. Match button positions swaps A/B and X/Y so they match the Xbox layout by position.
Turbo
Repeat a button while you hold it, or toggle it on and off, at the presses per second you choose.
Macros
A combination of up to three buttons plays a sequence of steps, each with its own hold and gap time.
Gyro
Aim with motion: as the right stick or as the mouse. Always on, while holding ZL, or toggled with R3, with sensitivity, smoothing and vertical invert.

Share and keep profiles

  • Profiles are .repad files. Export current or Export all saves them; Import from file…, dropping a file on the window, or opening a .repad file adds it.
  • Copy from another controller brings over a profile made for a different controller.
  • The profiles folder can be changed in Settings → General → Profiles.

Turbo, macros and gyro run in RelayPad on this PC. The controller itself is not changed.

NFC and amiibo

Open NFC and amiibo from the main window or the tray menu. It works with the controllers that have a reader:

  • Switch 2 Pro Controller, over Bluetooth or USB.
  • Right Joy-Con 2, alone or in a pair: hold the tag over its right stick. The left Joy-Con 2 has no reader.
  • Switch Pro Controller, over Bluetooth with the cable unplugged.

Read

Tap a tag and press Read tag, or turn on Read continuously. RelayPad shows the amiibo's character and game, or whether it is a blank tag, a tag with text or a locked tag. Recent reads stay in memory and can be exported as CSV.

Write and back up

  • Text is written only on blank or rewritable tags. RelayPad never writes over an amiibo, never writes a locked tag and never locks a tag.
  • Save backup keeps a copy of your own amiibo as a .bin file.
  • A backup can be written on a blank NTAG215, sealed like an original, with your own keys file. RelayPad never includes or downloads keys. See Back up your amiibo.

Writing an amiibo seals the tag for good. RelayPad names the amiibo and asks you to confirm first.

Tray menu

RelayPad lives in the Windows tray, next to the clock. Click its icon to open the menu:

Controllers
Each connected controller with its player and battery. Click a row to open RelayPad on that controller, or its gear to open its settings.
Profile
Switch the active profile, or go to Manage profiles…
Pause bridge
Unplugs the virtual controller until you choose Resume bridge.
NFC and amiibo
Shown when a connected controller has an NFC reader.
Activity log
Opens the log window.
Settings…
Opens the settings.
Start with Windows
Opens RelayPad minimized in the tray when you sign in.
Quit RelayPad
Closes RelayPad and its virtual controllers.

Settings

General
Startup and tray (start with Windows, close to tray, notifications, automatic updates, hide the controller from other apps), battery (low battery alert, threshold, battery in the tray), the profiles and amiibo folders, and diagnostics (logs folder, usage report).
Colors
The accent colors of the app: presets or your own.
Drivers
Install, reinstall or uninstall ViGEmBus and HidHide, see Bluetooth and USB, run diagnostics and copy the report.
About
Version, check for updates, source, releases, licenses and where to report an issue.

The language follows Windows, English or Spanish, and can be changed. The theme can be light, dark or follow Windows.

Updates

RelayPad looks for new releases every few hours and installs them when no controller is in use. Turn off Install updates automatically in Settings and it only tells you. Check for updates in About looks right away.

Troubleshooting

RelayPad tells you in the main window when something gets in the way, with what to do. The most common ones:

The game sees two controllers
Install HidHide from Settings → Drivers and turn on Hide the controller from other apps. On Switch 2 controllers this is still being tested.
Steam changes the buttons
Turn off Steam Input for Xbox controllers in Steam → Settings → Controller.
The USB controller is busy
Another app, such as Steam, holds it. Close that app or turn off its Nintendo controller support.
The controller does not show up
Hold sync again. A Pro Controller can also pair by plugging it in over USB once. Turn off a Switch console nearby.
A driver is missing
Install it from Settings → Drivers.
The stick drifts
Run Diagnose, then recalibrate the stick or raise its deadzone in the profile.
No rumble over Bluetooth
Rumble comes back when the controller reconnects.
The bridge stopped
Choose Resume bridge in the tray menu. If it keeps failing, restart Windows.

Logs and reporting a problem

The Activity log shows what happened in this session and keeps three files: events, power and errors. You can filter by controller, export it as CSV or clear it. The files live in %LOCALAPPDATA%\dev.relaypad.tray\logs; Settings → General → Logs folder opens it.

  • If RelayPad closes unexpectedly, the next start offers to Report on GitHub.
  • The errors log has the same button, for the whole log or for one line.
  • It opens a prefilled GitHub issue with a diagnostics report. Serial numbers and Bluetooth addresses are hidden, and nothing is sent until you submit the issue.

You can also open an issue yourself with a question or an idea.

Privacy

  • No account, no ads. RelayPad works offline.
  • The usage report, if you allow it, stays on this PC. It never holds a serial number or a Bluetooth address, and nothing is uploaded.
  • Amiibo backups and keys are never uploaded or logged.
  • The one network lookup is the amiibo name: when online, RelayPad asks amiiboapi.org once per amiibo ID.
  • A GitHub report is sent only when you submit it yourself.

Uninstall

Uninstall RelayPad from Windows Settings → Apps → Installed apps. The uninstaller removes the app, its shortcuts and its start-with-Windows entry, shows again any controller it hid, and removes RelayPad from HidHide's allowed programs.

  • Check Delete the application data to also remove settings, logs, profiles, amiibo backups and keys kept in RelayPad's own folders.
  • ViGEmBus and HidHide stay installed. Remove them first from Settings → Drivers if you no longer want them.
