Playing in three steps.

No accounts, no command lines, no editing files.

  1. 1

    Download and install

    Get the installer from the latest release and run it. RelayPad lives in your tray.

  2. 2

    Add the driver

    RelayPad installs ViGEmBus, the driver behind the virtual controller, in one click from Settings.

  3. 3

    Connect and play

    Plug in the cable or pair over Bluetooth. The app shows the exact steps for your model.

You need Windows 10 or 11 (x64) and a Bluetooth adapter or a USB cable.

Questions before you download.

Is RelayPad really free?

Yes. It is free for personal use, with no ads, no account and no trial period. Support is optional and unlocks nothing.

Windows warns about the installer. Is it safe?

RelayPad is new and its installer is not code signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may say "unknown publisher". Choose More info, then Run anyway. Updates are signed, and the app checks that signature before installing one. Every release also lists the SHA-256 of the installer and a VirusTotal scan.

Which games work?

Any game that supports an Xbox 360 or DualShock 4 controller, which covers almost every PC game with gamepad support, on Steam or anywhere else.

Will games see two controllers?

They can. The first-run setup offers the optional HidHide driver, also in Settings, Drivers; with it, games only see the RelayPad controller. On Switch 2 controllers this is still being tested.

Do I need Bluetooth?

Not always. The Switch 2 Pro Controller and the Switch Pro Controller work over a USB cable. Joy-Con 2 have no USB port, so they need a Bluetooth adapter.

Can I use several controllers at once?

Yes, up to four, each on its own player. You can move a controller to another player or swap two of them from the app.

Does it send my data anywhere?

No. RelayPad can keep a usage report on your PC if you allow it on first run, and nothing is uploaded. A GitHub report is sent only when you submit it yourself.

How do I uninstall it?

From Settings, Apps, Installed apps in Windows. It removes the app and its shortcuts, shows again any controller it hid, and can delete its settings, logs and profiles too. ViGEmBus and HidHide stay installed; remove them first from Drivers in RelayPad if you no longer want them.

How do I pair Joy-Con 2?

Detach them and hold the sync button on the rail until the lights sweep, with RelayPad open. Each one waits without a player: hold ZL and ZR together to join two as one controller, or press SL and SR on one to play it sideways.

Something does not work. How do I report it?

Open the activity log, or wait for the crash dialog on the next start, and choose Report on GitHub. It opens a prefilled issue with serial numbers and Bluetooth addresses hidden.

Does RelayPad update itself?

Yes. It looks for new releases every few hours and installs them when no controller is in use. You can turn that off in Settings and it only tells you.

Is there a version for Linux or Mac?

Not yet. RelayPad runs on Windows 10 and 11 today. A Linux version comes next, then macOS.

How can I support RelayPad?

On Ko-fi, once or monthly. It pays for the controllers RelayPad does not support yet, the Linux and macOS versions, and RelayPad's own driver. Starring the project on GitHub and telling a friend help too.

The controllers still missing

Each one has to be bought and tested by hand before it works natively in RelayPad:

  • Nintendo Switch Online GameCube Controller

Linux, then macOS

The same controllers on more computers: a Linux version next, then macOS, which needs a permission from Apple.

A driver of its own

Today games see the controller through ViGEmBus and HidHide, two drivers from other projects, and ViGEmBus is no longer developed. RelayPad's own driver removes that dependency.
